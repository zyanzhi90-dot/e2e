- **Research question**: In continuous soft-contact tasks, compared with existing simple contact representations, under which practical operating conditions do additional contact information—contact area, local geometry, tangential force or moment, and contact history, separately or in combination—improve optimal impedance decisions and held-out closed-loop outcomes, and what is the minimal sufficient contact representation required to achieve each improvement?
- **Observed phenomenon**: Finite contact area and local geometry can alter pressure and tracking; tangential force, friction, and moment capacity can constrain sliding contact; viscoelastic history can alter force and area over time; yet memoryless contact models, model-agnostic admittance, and static uncertainty-aware control can perform well in bounded regimes.
- **Evidence-backed phenomenon**: Accepted evidence supports the physical and control relevance of area, geometry, tangential wrench, and history in different settings, alongside strong simple-representation comparators, but no accepted study isolates when each component or combination changes the optimal impedance decision and held-out closed-loop result under matched resources.
- **Evidence status**: Accepted landscape evidence supports physical plausibility, adjacent control relevance, and a genuine unresolved component-by-condition decision-value map; the map and its minimal sufficient representations remain unmeasured.
- **Decisive evidence tier**: Decision-grade canonical Evidence Cards bound to the accepted Quality and Novelty verdicts, with decisive comparisons requiring held-out impedance actions and closed-loop outcomes rather than model-fit gains alone.
- **Measurement validity**: Use synchronized, independently calibrated six-axis wrench, robot pose or indentation, rendered impedance, sliding speed, contact-area and local-geometry estimates, and an independent task-quality signal; separate calibration from held-out specimens or regions; randomize repeated conditions; verify bandwidth, delay, drift, and estimator observability; and report transient and steady outcomes with uncertainty.
- **Artifact/confound alternatives**: Sensor drift, filtering, latency, saturation, gravity compensation, contact-area or geometry reconstruction error, coupling-medium or specimen change, robot compliance and tracking error, solver failure, friction or adhesion shifts, contact loss, estimator leakage, and unequal data, compute, objective, or safety budgets.
- **Independent support**: j4KJ1EdUyYAJ anchors memoryless optimal impedance; QPuQ4sfxSTIJ supports finite-area contact MPC; ZDySGaNsMn8J supports geometry, distributed pressure, and friction-aware contact control; USER-TASE-2023-3282974 anchors model-agnostic six-axis admittance; iasK6kEBzIEJ and _8NXx8-VkcgJ support low-order viscoelastic control relevance; Ubx3Xkv4y2kJ links history and evolving area to tangential wrench capacity; 9vRJMR2dgG4J anchors uncertainty-aware force MPC; USER-TUFFC-2011-1961 constrains identifiability.
- **Prevalence/effect scale**: Area, geometry, tangential loading, and time dependence recur across tissue indentation, robotic tissue force control, compliant grasping, ultrasound scanning, and force-motion MPC, but their separate and combined effects on optimal impedance decisions in continuous scanning are not quantified.
- **Scope and boundary**: Continuous sliding or scanning by a rigid probe over compliant finite-area environments; measurable normal and tangential wrench, pose or indentation, contact area, local geometry, speed, contact history, impedance actions, and task quality; excludes universal superiority claims, clinical efficacy, and universal tissue-injury thresholds.
- **Why it matters**: The answer determines which sensing and contact-model complexity is actually necessary for safe, effective impedance selection, where simple feedback is sufficient, and which closed-loop claims remain defensible under operating-condition shift.
- **Value if yes / value if no**: If extra information helps, identify the smallest observable component set and the regimes and outcomes for which it is required; if it does not, establish decision-equivalence of simpler calibrated or model-agnostic representations and avoid unnecessary sensing, identification, and computation.
- **Decision owner / threshold**: The scientific decision owner is the Quality and Novelty sequence followed by the human problem Gate. An added component earns inclusion only if, under matched data, compute, objective, and safety constraints, it changes selected stiffness or damping beyond implementation resolution and yields a repeatable held-out improvement whose confidence interval exceeds a prespecified sensor- and task-relevant equivalence margin on at least one primary safety or task endpoint without material loss on the others; a representation is minimal only if removing any included component destroys that result.
- **Plausible explanations**: Area or geometry becomes decision-relevant when patch shape, thickness, or curvature varies; tangential wrench becomes relevant near friction, slip, or moment-capacity limits; history becomes relevant when contact and relaxation timescales are comparable; interactions may make combinations useful where individual additions are not; or robust measured-wrench feedback may absorb all of these differences within practical margins.
- **Decisive probe or falsifier**: For each declared operating regime, matched simple baselines must select equivalent impedance and match richer representations within prespecified margins on held-out transient force or wrench, constraint violations, contact loss or slip, and independent task quality; every retained component must also pass observability, reproducibility, leave-one-component-out, and incremental decision-value tests.
- **Feasible discriminating probe**: Run a randomized repeated-measures benchtop study over phantoms spanning stiffness and relaxation, thickness and curvature, contact-patch changes, speed, direction, dwell, tangential load, and disturbances with withheld specimens; compare nested simple, area, geometry, tangential-wrench, history, and combination representations under equalized resources, followed by bounded ex-vivo confirmation.
- **Closest prior / residual delta**: The closest priors establish memoryless optimal impedance, finite-area and geometry/friction-aware MPC, model-agnostic admittance, viscoelastic identification, finite-area history mechanics, and uncertainty-aware force MPC, but do not provide the candidate's matched component-by-condition-by-outcome map or an observability- and decision-equivalence-based minimal sufficient representation.
- **Uncertainties**: Reliable online area and geometry estimation; tangential force or moment materiality; history-state identifiability; component interactions; justified impedance-action and outcome equivalence margins; independent scanning-quality endpoint; resource-matched estimator latency; and phantom-to-ex-vivo transfer.
