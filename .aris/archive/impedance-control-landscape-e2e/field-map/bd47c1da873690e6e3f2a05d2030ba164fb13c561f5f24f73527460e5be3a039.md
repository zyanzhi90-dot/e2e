## Consensus

```json
[
  "Impedance control is fundamentally control of a force-motion relation at an interaction port, not a synonym for soft position control.",
  "A single impedance architecture can span free and constrained motion, but impact establishment and maintained-contact stability are distinct questions.",
  "The commanded impedance and the physically realized impedance differ under model error, inner-loop dynamics, sensing, delay, flexibility, saturation, and sampling.",
  "Variable stiffness can inject energy; fixed-gain passivity cannot be assumed after scheduling or learning gains.",
  "Learning and adaptation methods should be classified by the object changed: robot-control coefficients for realizing a fixed impedance, selected target gains, repeated feedforward input, impedance schedule, task policy, learned reward, or energy-structured force field.",
  "An 'optimal' or 'transferable' impedance is objective- and protocol-relative: LQR optimality inherits its quadratic cost and LTI environment class, while IRL transfer in current evidence requires same-task forward-RL reoptimization rather than zero-shot cross-task reuse.",
  "Existing analytical guarantees are controller- and assumption-specific; empirical task success is not a substitute for port-level stability or safety evidence."
]
```
