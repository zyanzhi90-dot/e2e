- **Research question**: During continuous finite-area probe contact with soft and heterogeneous environments, under which measurable combinations of contact-history timescale, local geometry or contact-area change, sliding rate, and material shift does a scalar memoryless force-indentation/rate representation cease to be decision-equivalent to a stateful contact representation for selecting robot impedance, as judged by held-out impedance actions, force or wrench safety outcomes, and scanning-task outcomes?
- **Observed phenomenon**: Memory and creep can change force, pressure distribution, contact area, and tangential-moment capacity; low-order viscoelastic identification can improve calibrated tissue force control; finite-area models can improve tracking or reproduce area-dependent wrench; yet model-agnostic admittance and static-output GP-MPC can achieve nominal force regulation or conditional force constraints.
- **Evidence-backed phenomenon**: The accepted evidence establishes a contradiction between model-aware benefits in bounded transients or geometry-sensitive contact and strong simpler feedback or uncertainty-aware control, while no accepted study maps when richer contact state changes the impedance decision and closed-loop outcome under held-out continuous soft-contact shifts.
- **Evidence status**: Accepted landscape evidence supports physical plausibility, adjacent control relevance, and an unresolved decision-equivalence boundary; the boundary itself remains unmeasured.
- **Decisive evidence tier**: Decision-grade canonical Evidence Cards bound to the accepted quality and novelty verdicts, with the primary comparison requiring held-out closed-loop outcomes rather than open-loop fit alone.
- **Measurement validity**: Use synchronized, independently calibrated six-axis wrench, robot pose or indentation, commanded and rendered impedance, sliding speed, and an independent task-quality signal; separate calibration and evaluation specimens or regions; randomize repeated conditions; report transient and steady endpoints with uncertainty; verify sensor bandwidth and delay; and test state observability and stability before attributing outcome differences to contact memory.
- **Artifact/confound alternatives**: Sensor drift, filtering, latency, saturation, or gravity-compensation error; coupling-gel or specimen changes; robot compliance, tracking error, solver or saturation failure; curvature or registration error; estimator leakage or unequal data/compute budgets; perception shift; and friction, adhesion, or contact loss mistaken for viscoelastic relaxation.

- **Prevalence/effect scale**: Time dependence, finite contact area, and model mismatch recur across tissue indentation, robotic tissue force control, compliant grasping, ultrasound scanning, and force-motion MPC, but their effect specifically on continuous optimal-impedance scanning is not quantified; heterogeneous adjacent effects motivate estimating a regime boundary.
- **Scope and boundary**: Continuous sliding or scanning by a rigid probe over compliant finite-area environments, centered on robotic ultrasound-like contact and bounded to measurable force or wrench, indentation or pose, speed, contact history, and task-quality signals; excludes all soft-body dynamics, clinical efficacy, and universal tissue-injury thresholds.
- **Why it matters**: The answer determines whether contact-model complexity is necessary for safe and effective optimal impedance or is an unnecessary identification burden, and identifies which safety and task claims are defensible under model shift.
- **Value if yes / value if no**: If a boundary exists, identify the smallest control-relevant state and the regimes requiring it; if not, establish decision-equivalence of simpler calibrated or model-agnostic control and redirect effort to robust feedback, uncertainty calibration, and constraint design.
- **Decision owner / threshold**: The scientific decision owner is the problem-quality and novelty sequence followed by the human problem Gate. A stateful representation is necessary only if, under matched data, compute, objective, and safety constraints, it changes selected stiffness or damping beyond implementation resolution and yields a repeatable held-out improvement whose confidence interval exceeds a prespecified sensor- and task-relevant equivalence margin on at least one primary safety or task endpoint without material loss on the others.
- **Plausible explanations**: A low-dimensional relaxation and evolving-contact-area state becomes action-relevant when environment and dwell or sliding timescales are comparable; robust measured-wrench feedback may absorb most mismatch; reported richer-model benefits may reflect calibration, delay, friction, geometry, or comparator weakness; or only a smaller latent sufficient statistic may be useful because detailed parameters are weakly identifiable.
- **Decisive probe or falsifier**: A calibrated scalar memoryless or model-agnostic baseline must select equivalent impedance actions and match the stateful representation on held-out force or wrench, constraint violations, contact loss or slip, and independent task quality across declared shifts; added state must also pass observability, reproducibility, and incremental-prediction tests.
- **Feasible discriminating probe**: Run a bounded repeated-measures benchtop study over phantoms spanning relaxation times and stiffness, multiple curvatures or thicknesses, randomized sliding speeds and dwell histories, and withheld regions or specimens; compare model-agnostic feedback, scalar HC-like, finite-area memoryless, and low-order stateful representations under matched objectives and resources.
- **Closest prior / residual delta**: NNBO, AuSoScan, deformable-contact MPC, generalized-Maxwell identification, velocity-level PFC, and hybrid GP-MPC establish adjacent ingredients but do not test whether contact history or evolving finite-area state is minimally necessary for impedance selection under held-out continuous soft-contact shifts and decision-equivalent outcomes.
- **Uncertainties**: The best dimensionless timescale ratio; safe observability of contact area or pressure; persistence of action differences after resource matching; reliable scanning-quality endpoint; transfer from phantom to ex-vivo or in-vivo tissue; justified equivalence margins; and materiality of tangential or moment effects for the chosen probe.
